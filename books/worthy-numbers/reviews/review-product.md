# Worthy Numbers: product and growth review

Reviewer stance: senior product and growth lead, cold read, judging only the eleven pages.

## Verdict in short

The book reads well and looks finished. The cover carries the Worth Building look, the inside pages share one system, and every page ends on a usable "Use it" line. The book would earn trust.

As a funnel it is weak. Chef Mealan is named on the cover and the author page and then appears for real only on the back cover. Pages 3 to 9 never say where the reader's own number comes from. Page 8 is exactly where the app belongs, and the book skips it. The back cover then shows a breakfast the reader already fixed by hand on page 9. The call to action does not say what joining costs, what happens after the form, or why to join now. There is nothing for a reader who is not ready.

## Hook (question 1)

The cover stops a scroll: strong title block, a clear mark, a calm navy. The weak part is the promise. "The numbers on your food, and which ones help you decide" does not say decide what, or for whom. Nothing tells a person who trains or has a body goal that this book is for them, and those are the people Chef Mealan serves.

Page 2 is a plain contents table. The red PD row with "6.3" is the best hook on the page, but a cold reader cannot read it yet. The "what it says" column ("100 g", "20%", "1.0") gives away nothing. Page 2 should sell page 9.

## Findings, ranked by funnel impact

### 1. Page 8: the reader's own target has no source, and the app is missing where it fits best

- Now: "Your target comes from your day. 180 g of protein on 2,800 kcal is PD 6.4." The chart labels say "your target 6.4" and "target 6.4".
- Problem: 6.4 is one person's number shown as the reader's, and the page never says how a reader gets their own. That gap is the reason to try Chef Mealan.
- Change: Label the chart "Example target 6.4". Replace the second paragraph with: "That target belongs to someone who eats 180 g of protein on 2,800 kcal. Yours depends on your goal, your body and your training. Chef Mealan works it out with you and shows every step of the sum." This is the first in-page mention of Chef Mealan. It explains a gap, so it does not read as an ad.

### 2. Page 11: the back cover demo proves nothing the reader has not already done

- Now: "Your breakfast: 30 g granola with 200 ml milk 1.5%, PD 4.1. Chef Mealan adds 80 g of skyr, PD 6.5."
- Problem: Page 9 already added skyr to a granola bowl by hand. Here the bowl changes (30 g instead of 60 g) with no reason given, and the one thing only the app can do, the personal target with its working, is not shown.
- Change: Start from the page 9 bowl so the reader recognises it. Put the app's first output on top, in the app's own look: "Your target: PD [x]. Worked out from [goal], [weight] and [training days]. Tap to see the sum." Under it: "Your bowl: 60 g granola, 200 ml milk 1.5%, PD 3.4. Chef Mealan suggests: [real output from an app run], PD [y]." Use a real screenshot or a faithful crop of the open number view. Add one piece of proof: a real tester line with first name and permission, or "[N] people test Chef Mealan today."

### 3. Pages 5 and 8: PD looks like the claim the book just dismissed

- Now (page 5): "'High protein' is a share of the energy, not an amount." and "A share says nothing about your portion." Now (page 8): "PD: one number for protein against energy."
- Problem: PD is the same ratio as the EU claim, written in another unit (PD × 4 = percent of energy from protein). A careful reader sees the book dismiss a share on page 5 and sell one on page 8. PD also has no stated source, so it can look invented to sell the app.
- Change: Make the link the argument. Page 5 headline: "'High protein' means 20% of the energy comes from protein. That line is low." Page 5 Use it: "Read the claim as a first filter. Then check your portion against your own target on page 8." Page 8, new line after the definition: "PD is the protein claim from page 5 in another unit. PD 3 is the Source of protein line. PD 5 is the High protein line. The pack's line is the same for everyone. Your target comes from your day." Add one line of provenance: "Nutrition research calls this protein density."

### 4. Page 11: the call to action leaves out cost, timing and the reason for the code

- Now: "Get your personal code. Scan, or go to chefmealan.com. Leave your name and email, and you get a personal code to join."
- Problem: A cold reader does not know that Chef Mealan is in a closed test, whether it costs money, when the code arrives, or what device it runs on. A code request with no explanation reads as friction.
- Change: "Chef Mealan is in a closed test. Leave your name and email at chefmealan.com/book, and I send you a personal code within [N days]. It is free while the test runs, and it works [in the browser on any phone]." Reason to act now, only if true: "Testers shape what I build next. I read every piece of feedback myself." Keep the red box. Make the headline "Get your code to the closed test".

### 5. Page 11 and every page footer: the funnel cannot be measured, and the PDF loses its link when shared

- Now: The QR code and the text both point to "chefmealan.com". The page footers read "Worthy Numbers".
- Problem: Book traffic cannot be told apart from other homepage traffic, and a PDF read on a phone cannot scan its own QR code.
- Change: Point the QR code and the printed link to a dedicated path, for example chefmealan.com/book, that opens the code form directly and tags the source. Use a separate path for the GitHub copy. Make every printed URL a live link in the PDF. Change the running footer to "Worthy Numbers · chefmealan.com" so a forwarded page still carries the address.

### 6. Page 11: no next step for a reader who is not ready

- Now: The only action is the code form.
- Problem: Most readers of a lead magnet are not ready to try an app. With no smaller step, the book loses them.
- Change: Add one line under the CTA box: "If you want the next book first, tick Worth Eating on the same form." On page 8, give the hand method too: "To work out PD by hand: protein grams for the day ÷ kcal for the day × 100." Giving away the formula builds trust. The hard part is knowing your protein grams, and that is the app's job.

### 7. Page 9: the last content line is an aphorism where the bridge to the app should be

- Now: "The pack tells you about the food. Your grams and your day tell you about the meal." The PD row reads "From 3.4 to 6.3, against a target of 6.4" with no verdict.
- Problem: It is a symmetric aphorism closer, it breaks the house rules, and it leaves the reader unsure whether 6.3 is good enough.
- Change: "6.3 against a target of 6.4 is close enough for breakfast. Chef Mealan does this sum for every meal of your week. See the back cover."

### 8. Cover: the promise is vague and the audience is unnamed

- Now: "A short read" and "The numbers on your food, and which ones help you decide."
- Problem: The reader does not learn what they will be able to do, or that the book is about protein and their own meal.
- Change: Subtitle: "Six numbers on your food, and the one that tells you if your meal fits your day." Kicker: "THE WORTH SERIES · A 5-MINUTE READ". This also does series work (finding 15).

### 9. Page 2: the contents page does not hook

- Now: "One number on each page: what it tells you, and how to use it at your next meal." The column values are "100 g", "A to E", "20%", "1.0", "how fast, how much", "6.3".
- Problem: Page 2 is where a cold reader decides to go on, and the column values only make sense after reading the book.
- Change: Intro line: "Five of these numbers grade the food on the pack. The sixth checks your meal against your day. On page 9, one change takes a breakfast from PD 3.4 to 6.3." Rename the column "the catch" and fill it with short findings: "made for the shelf", "grades the product", "a low line", "not on any pack", "GL counts your portion", "3.4 to 6.3".

### 10. Page 10: the author page is too thin for a recruiter

- Now: "I have spent about 20 years building software products as a product manager, and I founded several startups along the way." and "I design and build Chef Mealan with AI, from the first sketch to every release".
- Problem: There is no domain, no outcome and no proof that the AI build is real. Recruiters scan for exactly those three.
- Change: "I'm a senior product manager in Munich. For about 20 years I have built B2B and B2B2C software products. I founded several startups and sold one of them, an event planning app. I design and build Chef Mealan with AI, from the first sketch to every release: [one proof, for example N releases since month, or the evals that check every number it shows]." Keep the product role in the first sentence, as it is now.

### 11. Page 10: "every recipe" promises something the book does not have

- Now: "I try every recipe and every number in this book on myself first."
- Problem: The book has no recipes. A reader who notices will doubt the rest of the page.
- Change: "I cook every day for my family, and I test every number in this book on my own meals first."

### 12. Page 10: no food credential for the reader

- Now: The only reasons to trust the author on food are cooking at home and testing on himself.
- Problem: A wellness reader wants one food credential. A recruiter loses nothing if it comes after the product lines.
- Change: After the Chef Mealan sentence, add: "I am also a certified fitness and nutrition coach." Keep it to one sentence, after the product role.

### 13. Page 8: the red dot makes granola look like a bad food

- Now: Granola sits on a red dot in a red zone. Chicken and rice and skyr sit on green dots.
- Problem: Red and green grade the food. The book's own argument, and the way Chef Mealan works, is that a number only means something against a person's day. The colour contradicts the text.
- Change: Draw all three dots in one neutral colour. Let the target line be the only accent, with a pale fill above it. The page then shows distance from a target, not good and bad food.

### 14. Page 5: the protein bar example fails its own rule

- Now: "Protein bar, 60 g, 250 kcal, 20%: High protein, about 12 g protein".
- Problem: 12 g × 4 kcal = 48 kcal, which is 19.2% of 250 kcal. That bar could not carry the High protein claim. A number reader will catch it on the page about checking numbers.
- Change: Make it "about 13 g protein" (52 kcal, 20.8%). Also replace the chart key "12% · 20% marked" with "Lines at 12% (Source of protein) and 20% (High protein)".

### 15. Page 11: the Worth series line is a footnote that does not place this book

- Now: "The Worth series: Worth Building is out now, and Worth Eating comes next."
- Problem: The line never says Worthy Numbers belongs to the series. "Worthy" also breaks the "Worth + word" pattern. Worth Eating, the book closest to this reader, gets no description and no way to get it.
- Change: "Worthy Numbers is a short read from the Worth series. Worth Building, a playbook for product people, is out now. Worth Eating, [one line on what it covers], comes next. Get it first with the same form." Optional: rename the book to fit the pattern, for example "Worth Counting". Test that title first, because "counting" can suggest calorie counting.

### 16. Page 11: the benefit list is in the wrong order and the craving line is unclear

- Now: "A week of meals planned with you, and the shopping list for it." / "A craving answered with the food that makes it fit your day." / "Every number open, so you see how Chef Mealan got to it."
- Problem: "Every number open" ties straight to the book's argument and is the differentiator, yet it comes last. "Makes it fit" is hard to parse on first read.
- Change: Put "Every number open" first. Craving line: "Name a craving, and Chef Mealan finds the food that makes it fit your day."

### 17. Pages 2 and 6: "on your food" is not true for every number

- Now: Page 2 says "Six numbers on your food". Page 6 says "You won't find these scores on a pack." The page 6 chart shows two bars and no food values.
- Problem: Protein quality and PD are not on any pack, and the chart shows a scale with nothing on it.
- Change: Page 2 title: "Six numbers about your food". On the page 6 chart, plot three foods on the DIAAS bar, for example milk about 1.1, soy about 0.9, wheat about 0.4. Check each value against your cited source before printing.

### 18. Page 7: GI is described as speed, and the headline is a symmetric pair

- Now: "GI says how fast. GL adds how much." and "by how fast they raise blood glucose in the two hours after eating".
- Problem: GI measures the size of the rise over two hours compared with pure glucose, not its speed. A nutrition-literate reader will flag it. The headline also breaks the no-symmetric-pairs rule.
- Change: Headline: "GL is GI with your portion in it." Body: "GI ranks carbohydrate foods from 0 to 100 by how much they raise blood glucose over two hours, compared with pure glucose."

### 19. Page 9: the Claim row does not say which bowl

- Now: "Claim: 25% of the bowl's energy is protein".
- Problem: 25% is true only with skyr. As poured, the bowl is 13%. This row is also the best place to tie back to page 5.
- Change: "As poured, 13%: a Source of protein. With skyr, 25%: past the High protein line."

### 20. Pages 3, 4 and 6: house rule breaks in the Use it boxes and body text

- Now (page 3): "At the shelf, compare per 100 g. At the table, multiply by your grams." Now (page 4): "Compare with the letter. Decide with your portion and your plate." Now (page 6): "does the protein bring enough of each, and how much of it does your body take up?"
- Problem: Pages 3 and 4 end on symmetric pairs. Page 6 builds its sentence as a question.
- Change: Page 3: "Compare packs per 100 g. For your bowl, multiply each value by your grams and divide by 100." Page 4: "Use the letter to pick between two products of the same kind. Then check your portion and the rest of your plate." Page 6: "A quality score checks two things: whether the protein brings enough of each, and how much of it your body takes up."

## Answers to the five questions

1. **Hook.** The cover design works. The promise and page 2 are too abstract to pull a cold reader through. Fix with findings 8 and 9.
2. **Funnel.** Chef Mealan appears only on the cover byline, the author page and the back cover. Add it in two places: page 8, where the reader's own target comes from (finding 1), and page 9, as the closing bridge (finding 7). Add the URL in the running footer (finding 5). That is enough, and none of it reads as an ad. The CTA is clear in mechanics but not worth acting on yet: it lacks proof (finding 2), the cost and timing (finding 4) and a step for readers who are not ready (finding 6).
3. **Author page.** It leads with the product role, which is right. It needs one domain, one outcome and one proof of the AI build for recruiters (finding 10), and one food credential for readers (finding 12). Remove the recipe claim (finding 11).
4. **Series.** The cover style carries the series well. The back cover treats the series as a footnote and never places this book in it (finding 15).
5. **Trust.** The risks are the protein bar maths (14), "your target" shown as someone else's number (1), PD without a source and against page 5 (3), GI described as speed (18), red for granola (13), and "every recipe" (11).

## The three changes I would make first

1. **Page 8:** label 6.4 as an example, say that a reader's own target depends on goal, body and training, and name Chef Mealan as the tool that works it out and shows the sum. Add the hand formula for readers who are not ready.
2. **Back cover:** rebuild the demo around the page 9 bowl and the personal target with its working, using real app output. Rewrite the CTA to say closed test, free, when the code arrives, and the Worth Eating option, and point it to a tracked chefmealan.com/book link.
3. **Pages 5 and 8:** show that PD is the EU protein claim in another unit (PD 3 and PD 5 are the claim lines) and that the reader's target replaces the pack's fixed line. This turns the book's weakest logical point into its strongest argument for the app.
