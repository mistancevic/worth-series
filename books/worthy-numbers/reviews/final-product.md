# Worthy Numbers: launch verdict (product and growth)

## 1. Launch verdict

Not ready yet, and close. The content is accurate, well paced and visually consistent: I rechecked every sum (pages 3, 5, 7, 8, 9) and all of them hold, and the charts are drawn to scale. Two things block launch. The author photo on page 10 is still a placeholder. And the only address for the code form sits on page 11, so a reader who stops early, or sees one page as a social image, never gets the URL. Fix those two, apply the top five below, and ship.

## 2. Hook

**Cover.** Strong title block, good contrast, and it reads at thumbnail size. The subtitle is the weak part. "The numbers about your food, and which ones help you decide" promises a topic, not a result. The book has a concrete result on page 9 (same granola, twice the protein), and the cover should promise it.

**Contents.** It works as a teaser because "six numbers" plus the red PD row signals a payoff. The "what it says" column is cryptic out of context ("1.0", "20%"). A scroller skims past it, which is acceptable for page 2.

**Page 3.** Clear and useful, though per 100 g versus portion is known ground for the fitness crowd this book targets. It earns the next page, it does not stop the scroll. The scroll stoppers are page 5 (a High protein bar loses to a pot of skyr) and page 9 (one swap doubles the protein). In a LinkedIn carousel, lead with those, not with page 3.

## 3. Funnel

**Reason to try.** Good build. Page 8 makes the reader do PD by hand, shows the example target 6.4, and says Chef Mealan works out your own target with you. Page 9 shows the payoff and says Chef Mealan does this sum for every meal of the week. The gap is real: the book gives an example target but no way to get your own, and the app fills that gap. That is the right engine.

**Back-cover ask.** Clear mechanics (scan or type, name and email, code within a day) and honest scarcity. Three weaknesses:
- The headline "Live the day, so it counts" is a slogan. It names no outcome, and it competes with the CTA box for attention.
- "Get your personal code" sells the code, not what the code unlocks (your own target and a planned week).
- The ask appears only once, on the last page.

**What raises the request rate:**
1. Put the URL at the moment of highest intent: the page 9 "Use it" box.
2. Put the URL in the footer of pages 3 to 9, so every page posted as an image carries it.
3. Rename the ask after the benefit, and write "I send your code". The hand check is a trust asset, so say it.
4. Tag the sources so you can see which channel converts: QR currently decodes to the plain `https://chefmealan.com/worthy-numbers`. Use `?src=qr` on the QR, `?src=pdf` on clickable PDF links, `?src=li` and `?src=ig` in posts. Make sure the URL is a live link in the PDF on pages 9 and 11.
5. If the spot count is real, state it ("[number] spots left in this round"). A number converts better than "limited".

## 4. Trust

- **Photo placeholder, page 10.** Blocker. A grey box on the author page reads as unfinished to both readers and recruiters.
- **Three different titles.** Cover: "Product and recipe builder". Page 10 body: "product and recipe builder". Page 10 footer: "Product builder". Pick one. For recruiters, the role should say senior product manager, and it should say it first. "Recipe builder" reads as a hobby next to a senior PM title.
- **"as a product manager" undersells.** Twenty years and founder exits point to senior, and the AI build line is the strongest recruiter signal on the page. Lead with both.
- **Claims a recruiter will check.** "Founded several startups and sold one" must match LinkedIn exactly. If LinkedIn shows two, write two.
- **Back-cover example lacks age and sex.** A coach or a nutrition-literate reader knows 2,350 kcal depends on both. "wants to keep his weight · 80 kg, 182 cm" → add "[age]" and "man".
- **No medical line.** Page 7 talks about blood glucose. Add one small line on page 11: "For adults 18+. Not medical advice: with diabetes or another condition, ask your doctor first." It also matches the app's 18+ rule.
- **No sources.** One small line on page 11 lifts credibility at no cost: "Sources: EU Regulations 1169/2011 and 1924/2006, FAO 2013 protein quality report, University of Sydney GI database."
- **Feature bullets on page 11.** Each one ("week of meals", "shopping list", "name a craving") must work for beta users today. A promise that fails in the first session costs more than a shorter list.
- **"I test every number in this book on my own meals first."** Nobody tests Nutri-Score or DIAAS on a meal. Narrow it: "I use these numbers on my own meals every day."

Checked and fine: EU claim thresholds (12%, 20%), PD conversions (PD 3, PD 5), DIAAS values, GL bands, Nutri-Score history, all bowl sums and percentages.

## 5. Social

**Post first: page 5** ("High protein" is a share of the energy, not an amount). It stands alone with no earlier page, the claim is counterintuitive, and the bar chart reads on a phone. Page 9 is the stronger second post, but it needs PD explained first.

**Caption:** A 250 kcal bar can say High protein with about 13 g of protein, while 200 g of skyr gives about 22 g for half the calories.

Put the link in the post body on LinkedIn, and in the bio link on Instagram.
