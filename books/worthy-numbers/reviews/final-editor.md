# Worthy Numbers: editor's verdict

## 1. Verdict

The book does its job. A cold reader learns which label numbers compare products and which ones decide a meal, and the guide hands over one tool (PD) with a worked bowl to prove it. Every number and sum checks out. The arc from "the label" to "one bowl, every number" is clean, and Chef Mealan appears as the natural next step, not a pitch bolted on. It is ready after five line fixes. The main gap: the example person behind "target 6.4" only appears on the back cover, so pages 8 and 9 ask the reader to trust a number from a stranger.

## 2. Structure and flow

- **Order is right.** Pages 3 to 7 move from product-level numbers (label, letter, claim) to body-level ones (quality, GI/GL), then page 8 lands on the one number that decides, and page 9 applies all six. Moving PD up next to page 5 would kill the build. Keep it.
- **Each page earns its place.** Page 6 is the weakest for deciding a meal, since no pack prints DIAAS. It still earns its spot: it warns that grams of protein are not all equal, right before the book asks you to count them.
- **Page 4 says one thing three times.** "The letter does one job: comparing products of the same kind", the "It helps" box and the Use it box all repeat it. Cut the body sentence (see smaller fixes).
- **PD thread holds.** Page 5 points forward ("Page 8 turns this share into one number"), page 8 points back ("the protein claim from page 5 in another unit"), page 9 runs 3.4 to 6.4, and page 11 closes on "PD 6.4" with the person it belongs to. Two weak links: page 8 states PD 3 and PD 5 without showing how 12% becomes 3, and the example person is missing until page 11. Changes 1 and 2 fix both.
- **Page 9 GI and GL row** gives no number ("Most carbohydrate comes from the granola"). Acceptable, since the page title promises every number, not every value. Leave it.

## 3. Where a cold reader gets lost

1. Page 8: "Source of protein is PD 3, and High protein is PD 5." The reader cannot get from 12% to 3 without knowing protein gives 4 kcal per gram. The book never says it.
2. Page 9: "PD 6.4 meets the example target, so this bowl fits. Aim near your target at each meal, since real days never come out exact." The target came from a daily total (150 g on 2,350 kcal). Nothing explains why a daily number applies to one bowl. The reason (PD is a ratio) is the key idea of the book and it is unstated.
3. Page 9, PD row: "From 3.4 to 6.4, on the example target, just by replacing one food." "On the example target" reads as "measured against" rather than "reaching". The reader also has to work out which food.

- Page 6: "measures each amino acid at the end of the small intestine, where your body takes it up." Uptake happens along the small intestine; the end is where DIAAS measures what is left. Small fix below.
- Page 8 chart: "rice weighed cooked" but chicken has no state. "About 10" holds only for cooked chicken.

## 5. House-rule breaks

No en or em dashes, no question marks, no banned words, no -ly intensifiers. Remaining:

- **Passive, page 8:** "One example, worked out by Chef Mealan". Fixed by change 2.
- **Passive, page 10:** "the foundation Chef Mealan is built on". Fixed by change 5.
- **Filler, page 9:** "just by replacing one food". Fixed by change 4.
- **Repeated "X, not Y" headlines:** pages 3, 4 and 5 in a row ("per 100 g, not per bowl", "the product, not your meal", "a share of the energy, not an amount"). Each states its page thesis, so each is allowed alone. Three in a row reads as a template. Break page 5 (smaller fixes).
- **Mirrored pair, page 4:** the "It helps / It can't" boxes. It is a design device and it carries real content. Keep it, but it is the reason to cut the body sentence that repeats it.
- **Slogan, page 11:** "Live the day, so it counts". It is a cover line, not a closer. Acceptable. "Chef Mealan supports you on that journey" is filler (smaller fixes).

## 6. Top five changes

**1. Page 8, second paragraph** (fixes the 12% to PD 3 jump)
- Current: "PD is the protein claim from page 5 in another unit. Source of protein is PD 3, and High protein is PD 5. Those lines are the same for everyone."
- Replace: "PD is the protein claim from page 5 in another unit. Protein gives 4 kcal per gram, so Source of protein (12%) is PD 3, and High protein (20%) is PD 5. Those lines are the same for everyone."

**2. Page 8, third and fourth paragraphs** (puts the person behind 6.4 where the number appears; removes a passive and one of two Chef Mealan mentions; the fourth paragraph goes because page 11 opens with the same point and the page needs the room)
- Current: "Your own target comes from your goal, your body and your training. One example, worked out by Chef Mealan: 150 g of protein on 2,350 kcal is PD 6.4. Chef Mealan works out your own target with you and shows every step of the sum. / PD is one helper. Energy, your other nutrients, movement and sleep count too."
- Replace: "Your own target comes from your goal, your body and your training. One example: a man of 80 kg who wants to keep his weight and trains twice a week needs about 150 g of protein on 2,350 kcal a day. That makes his target PD 6.4. Chef Mealan works out your own target with you and shows every step of the sum."

**3. Page 9, Use it** (states why a daily target works for one bowl)
- Current: "PD 6.4 meets the example target, so this bowl fits. Aim near your target at each meal, since real days never come out exact. Chef Mealan does this sum for every meal of your week, against your own target."
- Replace: "PD 6.4 meets the example target, so this bowl fits. PD is a ratio, so one daily target works for a snack and for a full plate. Chef Mealan does this sum for every meal of your week, against your own target."

**4. Page 9, PD row**
- Current: "From 3.4 to 6.4, on the example target, just by replacing one food"
- Replace: "From 3.4 to 6.4, the example target, by swapping milk for skyr"

**5. Page 10, third paragraph** (removes a passive, the stacked appositives and the jargon "agentic"; the coach credential gets its own sentence)
- Current: "I’m a certified fitness and nutrition coach too, and I wrote Worth Building, a playbook on product thinking for the AI and agentic era, the foundation Chef Mealan is built on."
- Replace: "I’m a certified fitness and nutrition coach. I also wrote Worth Building, a playbook on product thinking in the age of AI. Chef Mealan follows its method."

## Smaller fixes, if time allows

- Page 4: cut "The letter does one job: comparing products of the same kind." and start the next sentence "The letter can’t see how much you eat, what you eat it with or what your day needs."
- Page 5 headline: "“High protein” is a share of the energy, not an amount." becomes "“High protein” is a share of the energy." The body already says it gives no grams.
- Page 6: "measures each amino acid at the end of the small intestine, where your body takes it up." becomes "measures what is left of each amino acid at the end of the small intestine, so it shows how much your body took up."
- Page 7: "A GL of 10 or less counts as low, and 20 or more as high." becomes "A GL of 10 or less counts as low, 11 to 19 as medium, and 20 or more as high." The chart already says "medium".
- Page 8 chart label: "rice weighed cooked" becomes "chicken and rice weighed cooked".
- Page 10 sign-off: "Product builder, and the maker of Chef Mealan" becomes "Product and recipe builder, and the maker of Chef Mealan", to match the cover and the first line of the page.
- Page 10: "B2B and B2B2C software products" becomes "software products". A food-label reader does not need the acronyms.
- Page 11: "Chef Mealan supports you on that journey." becomes "Chef Mealan plans the food part of that day with you."
